# Module 03: Go Deeper with Deep Research

> **Business Context:** Jordan knows *what* is underperforming — but the buyer meeting requires understanding *why*. "Sales are down" isn't a negotiating position. "Sales are down because of a supply disruption at our third-largest supplier, compounded by a seasonal shift we missed" is. Deep Research produces that level of insight in minutes, not days.

**Time:** ~15 minutes

---

## 🎯 Quick Summary

- **Deep Research** is an investigation mode for complex, open-ended questions
- It decomposes your question into multiple sub-investigations and runs them in parallel
- It synthesizes findings into a structured, cited report — every claim traces back to source data
- Investigations can take up to 10 minutes (typically 3–5 for most questions)
- The resulting report stays in context — you can ask follow-up questions without re-running
- **Artifacts** let you save charts, tables, and analyses for later reference or sharing

---

## 🧭 What You'll Do

1. Launch a Deep Research investigation
2. Watch the multi-agent decomposition in real time
3. Review a cited, structured report
4. Save key findings as Artifacts
5. Ask follow-up questions on the report

---

## 🛠️ Step 1: Launch Deep Research

In Module 02, you identified an underperforming subcategory. Now you need to understand the root cause. This is exactly what Deep Research is designed for.

Click the **+ button** in the message bar, then select **Deep Research**.

<!-- SCREENSHOT: + menu showing Deep Research option -->

Type the following question:

```
Why has the [Cookware/your declining subcategory] subcategory been declining over the past 6 weeks? Consider supply chain factors, pricing changes, seasonal patterns, and competitive dynamics.
```

> **Tip:** Replace `[Cookware/your declining subcategory]` with whichever subcategory you identified as declining in Module 02.

Press Enter to start the investigation.

---

## 🛠️ Step 2: Watch the Investigation

Deep Research doesn't just answer your question — it investigates it. Watch the progress indicator:

<!-- SCREENSHOT: Deep Research progress showing sub-investigations -->

You'll see CoWork:
1. **Decompose** your question into 3–5 sub-investigations (e.g., "Analyze supplier delivery timelines," "Check pricing history," "Compare seasonal patterns to last year")
2. **Execute** each sub-investigation in parallel, querying different data sources
3. **Synthesize** the findings into a cohesive narrative

This typically takes 3–5 minutes. While it runs, you'll see status updates showing which sub-investigations are in progress.

> **When to use Deep Research vs. standard chat:**
>
> | Use Deep Research when... | Use standard chat when... |
> |--------------------------|--------------------------|
> | You need to understand *why* | You need a quick *what* |
> | The answer spans multiple data domains | The answer comes from one table/metric |
> | You want a shareable report with citations | You want a quick number or chart |
> | You'd normally ask an analyst for this | You'd normally check a dashboard for this |

---

## 🛠️ Step 3: Review the Report

When the investigation completes, CoWork presents a structured report. Review it carefully:

<!-- SCREENSHOT: Deep Research report with sections and citations -->

The report typically includes:
- **Executive summary** — 2-3 sentence headline finding
- **Detailed findings** — organized by the sub-investigations it ran
- **Citations** — every claim links back to the specific data query or source
- **Recommended actions** — what to do next based on the analysis

**Look for citations.** Each finding is tagged with a reference (e.g., `[1]`, `[2]`) that you can click to see the underlying data or query. This is the traceability that lets you say in your buyer meeting: "Here's exactly where this insight comes from."

---

## 🛠️ Step 4: Save an Artifact

The report contains valuable insights you'll want to reference later — especially in your 2pm buyer meeting. Let's save it.

Look for the **Save as Artifact** button (or pin icon) on the report, or specific charts within it.

<!-- SCREENSHOT: Artifact save button on a report or chart -->

Click it to save. Give it a meaningful name:

```
Home & Kitchen Decline Analysis - [Today's Date]
```

**What are Artifacts?**
- A persistent chart, table, or report that CoWork generates
- Saved to your workspace — accessible from the left panel
- Shareable with colleagues (within their access permissions)
- Revisitable without regenerating — the analysis is preserved

> **Key difference:** Without Artifacts, you'd need to re-ask the question to see the same analysis again. With Artifacts, your best analyses are always one click away.

## 🛠️ Step 5: Ask Follow-Up Questions

The Deep Research report stays in your conversation context. You can now ask follow-up questions without re-running the full investigation:

**Follow-up 1:**
```
Based on this analysis, which suppliers should I prioritize discussing in my buyer meeting today?
```

**Follow-up 2:**
```
Summarize the top 3 findings in bullet points I can put on a slide
```

**Follow-up 3:**
```
What data would I need to track weekly to catch this kind of decline earlier?
```

<!-- SCREENSHOT: Follow-up response with actionable summary -->

CoWork uses the full report context to give you precise, relevant answers — no need to repeat background information.

## ✅ Best Practices

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Use Deep Research for "why" questions that span multiple domains | Use it for quick metric lookups (standard chat is faster) |
| Give context in your question (time period, factors to consider) | Ask vague questions like "why are things bad?" |
| Check citations before sharing findings with stakeholders | Present Deep Research findings without verifying the source |
| Save reports as Artifacts for your records | Rely on finding the same conversation thread days later |
| Ask follow-up questions to refine findings | Re-run the entire investigation for a minor clarification |

## 🌐 Why This Matters

You just produced a multi-source, cited analysis in under 5 minutes that would typically require:
- A data analyst (2-3 days to queue and execute)
- A business analyst (1 day to synthesize and write up)
- A review meeting (to validate findings)

**Total traditional time: 3-5 business days. Your time: 5 minutes.**

And critically — the report is *cited*. Every finding traces to data. This isn't a hallucination or a guess; it's an auditable analysis you can defend in a buyer meeting, a board presentation, or an executive review.

This is what "Deep Analysis, Real Action" means: not just surfacing numbers, but producing the *understanding* that drives decisions.
